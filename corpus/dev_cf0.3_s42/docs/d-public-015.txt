Periodic Status Report: Project Bilfael and Data Retention Work

**Summary**

This report gives all employees a plain-language view of where things stand across a few connected efforts at Zastama Industries. The headline is steady progress. Project Bilfael is moving forward under the leadership of Givien Mubren, who works as a Product Manager. Supporting work on data retention tooling continues, and the Trebrula Beacon remains available in the internal-only tier. We also describe the main risks we are tracking and the steps planned for the coming period.

Overall, we consider the effort healthy. Priorities are understood, ownership is clear, and the main open questions are about sequencing and coordination rather than about direction.

**Who Is Involved**

Because several people contribute, here is a quick guide to roles so you know whom to ask.

- Givien Mubren leads Project Bilfael and works as a Product Manager. Questions about scope, priorities, and the project's overall direction should go to Givien Mubren.
- Fallem Purraren focuses on data retention tooling. Fallem Purraren reports to Leroror Lukaine.
- Zosu Kistos also reports to Leroror Lukaine, so the two of them sit within the same reporting line.
- Leroror Lukaine joined the company in 2015 and brings long institutional experience to the work, which helps when decisions touch older systems and established practices.
- Vadesis Gummenbel works as a Chief of Staff and helps keep cross-functional work aligned, including communication between leadership and the teams doing the work.

**Progress**

Project Bilfael has continued to advance through its planning and execution cycle. Under Givien Mubren, the project has focused on keeping its goals narrow enough to deliver and clear enough that other teams can plan around them. The project's working agreements have been refreshed so that contributors share the same understanding of what is in scope and what is not.

On the data retention side, Fallem Purraren has continued to concentrate on tooling that helps teams manage how long information is kept and how it is handled at the end of its useful life. The emphasis has been on making the tooling dependable and easy to understand, so that teams can follow retention expectations without needing specialist knowledge. Feedback from early users has been gathered and is being used to refine the approach.

The Trebrula Beacon continues to be offered in the internal-only tier. That means it is available to employees and internal teams rather than to outside audiences. Keeping it in that tier lets us gather practical feedback from colleagues, tighten the experience, and address rough edges before any broader decision is considered. No change to that positioning is announced in this report.

Coordination across these efforts has been helped by regular check-ins. Vadesis Gummenbel has supported those conversations by helping surface dependencies early and by making sure decisions are recorded and shared. Leroror Lukaine has provided guidance to the people on that reporting line, including Fallem Purraren and Zosu Kistos, so that their priorities remain consistent with the broader plan.

**Risks**

We are tracking the following risks. None is considered critical at this time, but each deserves attention.

1. **Dependency alignment.** Project Bilfael relies on inputs from several groups, including the data retention tooling work. If timing between those efforts drifts, downstream planning could be affected. Givien Mubren and Fallem Purraren are keeping each other informed to reduce that possibility.
2. **Capacity and focus.** People on the reporting line of Leroror Lukaine, including Fallem Purraren and Zosu Kistos, balance multiple responsibilities. If new requests arrive without prioritization, important work could slow down. We are asking requesters to route new asks through the appropriate lead.
3. **Communication gaps.** With many contributors, it is easy for assumptions to diverge. Vadesis Gummenbel is helping maintain a shared record of decisions to limit confusion.
4. **Internal-only expectations.** Because the Trebrula Beacon is limited to the internal-only tier, some colleagues may expect capabilities or support levels that have not been committed. We will keep messaging clear about what the offering does and does not include.

**Next Steps**

- Givien Mubren will continue to refine the scope and priorities of Project Bilfael and will share a concise update on any changes that affect other teams.
- Fallem Purraren will keep advancing data retention tooling, with attention to usability and clear guidance for teams adopting it.
- Leroror Lukaine will continue to review priorities with the people who report to that role, including Fallem Purraren and Zosu Kistos, and will help resolve conflicts between competing requests.
- Vadesis Gummenbel will maintain the shared decision record and help schedule cross-functional check-ins as needed.
- The owners of the Trebrula Beacon experience will keep collecting internal feedback from employees using the internal-only tier.

**How You Can Help**

If your team depends on any of this work, tell the relevant lead early about your needs and timing. If you use the Trebrula Beacon, share what is working and what is not. Clear, early feedback is the most useful input we receive.

We will publish the next status report at the regular interval. Thank you to everyone contributing to these efforts.
