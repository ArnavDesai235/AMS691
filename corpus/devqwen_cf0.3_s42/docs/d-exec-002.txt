Project Pirpoan Status Report – Q3 Update

Zastama Industries’ Project Pirpoan has continued to advance steadily this quarter, with a focus on aligning identity and access management systems across the integration scope. The Rishalen Integration Team, which holds the vault key ID RH-4278-L, has been central to this effort, ensuring secure and controlled access for all stakeholders. The project remains within its approved budget of $5.5M, and no financial deviations have been reported to date.

The Rishalen Integration Team is responsible for identity and access tooling.

The Rishalen Integration Team has completed the initial phase of tooling configuration, successfully deploying the first set of identity verification protocols. These protocols are now being tested in a controlled environment, and early feedback has been positive. The team has also initiated a cross-departmental coordination effort to ensure that all access points are properly documented and aligned with current security policies.

Project Pirpoan’s core development modules are progressing along expected timelines, with the first three out of five major deliverables either completed or under review. The finance lead is currently finalizing the second-phase allocation, which will be used to cover upcoming integration testing and stakeholder training. The Rishalen Integration Team has begun working closely with the training division to prepare materials that will be used during the onboarding phase for new users.

A number of risks remain under active monitoring. The most pressing is the potential delay in final vendor confirmation for the upcoming system certification phase. While the vendor has not provided a concrete timeline, discussions are ongoing. Additionally, the Rishalen Integration Team has flagged a need for increased oversight in managing access permissions, particularly as the number of active users continues to grow.

Ongoing risk management is focused on maintaining visibility into integration dependencies and ensuring that all teams are aligned with evolving compliance standards. Internal audits are being scheduled to evaluate the effectiveness of current access governance models, and contingency plans are being developed for potential bottlenecks in the testing phase.

Looking ahead, the next steps for Project Pirpoan include finalizing the access governance model by the end of the quarter, initiating a full-scale system test in early Q4, and preparing for the first round of user training sessions. The Rishalen Integration Team will continue to coordinate with internal stakeholders to ensure that all security and compliance requirements are met ahead of the test phase. A follow-up review will be conducted at the next internal alignment meeting, where the updated risk assessment will be presented for approval.

The team remains confident that, with the current trajectory and ongoing collaboration, Project Pirpoan will meet its Q4 launch objective.
