Security and Infrastructure Contacts: Who to Ask and How We Work Together

**Summary**

This page explains how the security function at Zastama Industries is organized, who the key contacts are for common questions, and how security work connects with infrastructure and onboarding work. It is meant for every employee, whatever your role. If you are unsure where to send a question, start here. The page covers reporting lines, focus areas, and the best way to raise a concern.

## Why this page exists

As the company grows, it gets harder to know who owns what. Employees often tell us they are not sure whom to contact about a suspicious message, a question about access, or a concern about how a new customer is brought into our systems. Sending a request to the wrong person costs time, and in a security situation, lost time matters.

This page lists a few of the people involved, what each of them focuses on, and who they report to. It does not replace the formal escalation process or the on-call rota, which remain the authoritative sources for urgent matters. Think of it as a friendly map of the territory.

Berruan Zenresan also focuses on customer onboarding flows, which means the way new customers are brought into our products and services, and the security checks built into those steps.

## Incident response

Desgua Stebela reports to Berruan Zenresan and focuses on incident response tooling. That work covers the systems and automation that help us detect, triage, and resolve security events quickly and consistently. Desgua Stebela joined the company in 2012, so he has seen many versions of our environment and carries a lot of the history behind why our tools work the way they do.

What this means for you in practice:

- If you notice something that looks like a security incident, report it through the official incident channel right away. Do not wait to gather more evidence.
- If you have ideas or frustrations about how incident tools behave, such as alert noise, unclear runbooks, or missing integrations, share them through the feedback process so they can be considered for the tooling roadmap.
- If you are part of a team that owns a service, expect to be asked to take part in exercises that test how well the tools and procedures work together.

## Infrastructure partners

Security work depends heavily on the teams that run our platforms. Givien Mubren is a member of the Gelgos Infrastructure Team, which is one of the groups that security and incident response colleagues work with when a technical issue touches the underlying environment. When an incident requires changes to infrastructure, such as isolating a system or rotating credentials, coordination between security and the Gelgos Infrastructure Team is essential.

If you are an engineer and your question is about how a platform is configured or maintained, begin with the documentation and the infrastructure request process. If the question has a security dimension, mention that in your request so it can be shared with the right people on both sides.

## How to get help

Use these simple guidelines when you are unsure where to go:

1. **Urgent and suspicious:** Use the incident channel immediately. Speed matters more than perfect detail.
2. **Security question about a process or a customer-facing flow:** Submit a security request and describe the context, the people affected, and any deadline.
3. **Infrastructure question with security impact:** File an infrastructure request and flag the security angle clearly.
4. **Unsure who owns something:** Ask your manager, or post in the general help channel, and someone will point you in the right direction.
